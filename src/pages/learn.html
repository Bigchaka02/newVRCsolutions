What is a Certificate of Analysis?

A Certificate of Analysis (COA) is a lab report that tells you what’s actually in a vial — and what isn’t. For research peptides, a proper COA answers three questions: identity (is this really the peptide you ordered?), purity (how much of the vial is the target compound versus impurities?), and safety (is it free of endotoxins and microbial contamination?).

VRC certificates come from independent third-party labs (Freedom Diagnostics or Janoshik Analytical). The COA link on a product page shows the document for the batch you’re receiving — not a stock image or a supplier certificate. Where a certificate is still pending, the product page and the COA library say so.

How to read a peptide COA

A peptide COA typically has four sections. Here’s what to look for:

  • Identity (Mass Spectrometry / MS): The observed molecular mass should match the theoretical mass for the peptide sequence, within a few daltons. If MS doesn’t match, the vial isn’t what the label says it is.
  • Purity (HPLC): A chromatogram with a single large peak. The area under that peak, divided by total peak area, is the purity percentage. VRC targets ≥99% on every peptide.
  • Water content (Karl Fischer): Peptides are hygroscopic. Excess water means less actual peptide per mg. Below 8% is standard.
  • Endotoxin (LAL): Bacterial toxin residue. Look for <0.5 EU/mg.

Purity, endotoxin, and microbial testing explained

Purity tells you how much of the powder is actually the peptide you ordered versus synthesis by-products or truncated sequences. A 99% purity peptide has 1% “other stuff” — usually shorter peptide fragments that fell off during synthesis. Higher purity means more predictable results in whatever assay you’re running.

Endotoxins are fragments of bacterial cell walls (lipopolysaccharides). Even trace amounts can trigger inflammatory responses in cell cultures, ruining your experiment. The LAL (Limulus amebocyte lysate) test measures endotoxin concentration in Endotoxin Units per mg. Anything under 0.5 EU/mg is considered research-grade clean.

Microbial testing (PCR-based screens) checks for bacterial and fungal contamination. Not every peptide COA includes this — it’s more common on liquid formulations. We include microbial data where our supplier provides it.

Why “Research Use Only” matters

All VRC products are labeled Research Use Only (RUO). That designation isn’t legal fine print — it defines what the products are for and what they are not.

  • Not for human or veterinary use. These compounds have not been evaluated by the U.S. FDA for safety or efficacy in humans or animals.
  • Not for diagnostic or therapeutic use. They are not drugs, supplements, or medical devices.
  • Intended for laboratory research. In vitro studies, method development, analytical reference standards, and other lab work.

By purchasing from VRC, you affirm you are a qualified researcher or laboratory operating in a jurisdiction where the possession and study of research chemicals is legal, and that you will use the products consistent with the RUO designation. If that’s not you, we’re not the right supplier — and you shouldn’t order.
